ASLPrep · head-motion correction probe

HMC Probe Findings

What drives the spurious motion in ASLPrep's head-motion correction, and whether any alternative does better, tested on simulated data with known truth and on the aslprep-zoo panel.

Findings so far

  • The spurious label motion is systematic. It is there with the noise switched off and barely changes with ten times the noise. It comes from registering one contrast to another. The GE path (delta-M to M0 through a smoothed reference) is the exception: its offset is the largest without noise, and noise makes it worse.
  • No MCFLIRT cost function fixes it. Registering each volume type to its own template does.
  • Blurred references bias registration. A sharp image registered to a blurred copy of itself moves; matched blur removes it. This contributes to the GE path's errors (#702) and penalizes averaged templates whenever the head moves.
  • Production is the best estimator on average on simulated motion (median edge error 0.10). Own-type templates win on still heads and lose with motion (0.35 and 0.39). The margins are small, often under 0.1 mm.
  • Production fails badly in the tail on real data. With a 5% tie margin on the shared-mask energy, c1-pld and c2-volreg beat production on about a tenth of real runs and lose on 1–2%. The runs they win are mostly production failures, not small gains (ds007116 sub-21035: production FD 10 mm, C2 0.15 mm). That argues for keeping production's estimator with a failure guard and a fallback, not for keeping it unchanged.
  • Saturation-recovery normalization (C5, #673) ties production. It refuses ineligible runs correctly. A voxelwise T1eff fit is biased toward its own pose estimates; a per-slice fit avoids that but does not beat production.
  • A joint contrast and motion model (C6) is identifiable but does not beat production. An exact identifiability test shows that the perfusion image must be zero outside the brain, or it absorbs label motion. The GPU prototype also needs the perfusion image bounded, and is still limited by how far the simulator departs from its model. It helps only on motion confounded with volume type, where C2 is already as good.
  • No short-listed estimator passes gate 2. Each improves what it targets (pair alignment, reference offset) but is worse end to end with motion, in the harness and through full ASLPrep.
  • C9, production with its label offset removed, beats production on both still heads and motion. It keeps production's transforms and composes into the labels of each delay one rigid correction that brings the corrected label mean onto the control mean; early, arterial delays borrow the nearest other delay's estimate (C9-auto). Median edge delta-M error with motion falls from 0.29 to 0.09; through full ASLPrep its CBF error is lower than production's on 12 of 14 fixtures (a third lower on still heads, more than half with motion). It is the only arm better than production on both. A first version (one offset from the later delays) regressed in the PASL families on held-out simulations; C9-auto passes a fresh held-out set (better on 15 of 18, no family regressing).
  • On real data C9-auto does no harm by the motion-sensitive metrics (delta-M energy worse than production's on none of 260 runs, better on 15%; negative fraction unchanged), but fails the pre-registered split-half criterion in two small families (3D EPI at 7 T, 17 runs from one dataset; 3D GRASE multi-delay, 7 runs), where the paired per-run change is -0.005.
  • Decision: C9-auto is in ASLPrep as an opt-in method, --hmc-method typewise_offset, with production (typewise) still the default. The integrated version reproduces the probe's CBF results exactly on the three fixtures checked end to end, and passed an adversarial and an ordinary Codex review. It keeps production's reference, so masks, coregistration and M0 registration are production's; what stands between it and the default is the real-data split-half criterion in two small families.
  • Reusing derivatives now works and is all-or-nothing. The atlas datasets no longer wipe precomputed transforms (PennLINC/aslprep#738), products are reused only with what they were made from, and transforms record the HMC method they were made with. A run given its own outputs reproduces them exactly.
  • ASLPrep ignores precomputed transforms whenever atlases are enabled (found wiring the candidates in): the atlas datasets are collected last and replace the transforms.
  • The simulator favours production. aslscan moves otherwise clean images rigidly, with no spin history and high SNR: the conditions per-type MCFLIRT is built for. The approaches of mature pipelines (zig-zag regression, saturation recovery) target effects the simulator barely produces, and C8 reimplements only ExploreASL's and ASLtbx's motion step, not their whole pipelines.
  • Zig-zag regression, as in ASLtbx and ExploreASL, is perfect on still heads and harmful with real motion (median 1.37). Spikes alias onto the alternation (a Huber fit fixes that), and drift is removed with it (nothing fixes that). Their exact parameter convention changes nothing.
  • AFNI 3dvolreg is the most precise engine for same-contrast templates. With it, C2's pair alignment matches or beats production's, but its edge delta-M error is still higher, so no C1/C2 variant beats production on subtraction yet.
  • A harness bug, found on real data, is fixed. The single-image registrations used FLIRT's global search, which put a spurious 1.5–2° offset on every label volume of C1/C2 on real data; every affected run was redone with MCFLIRT. The split-half correlation rated the broken runs best, so it is not read alone.
  • ASLPrep's 1 mm GRASE coregistration error is a BBR sign problem. White matter is brighter than grey in that reference, BBR fails and the run falls back to its initialization; the opposite sign registers it to under 0.5 mm.
  • The same sign problem explains #503 in simulation. On a stack-of-spirals fixture, ASLPrep's coregistration misses by 10 mm without triggering the fallback. The opposite BBR sign, or a perfusion image as the reference, brings it under 0.4 mm. Vessels and a perfusion deficit do not break the perfusion reference, but the conventional one is better on those fixtures.

Is the spurious motion noise?

On a still head every estimated transform should be the identity. Production registers label volumes to a reference built from other volumes (control, or a separate M0 scan), and the label volumes come out displaced anyway. The question for #705 and #707 is whether that offset is noise the registration chases or something systematic.

The simulator can switch noise off. With no noise at all the label offset is already there: 0.049 mm on pcasl1pld and 0.38 mm on 3D GRASE. On the control-label fixtures, adding noise up to ten times the calibrated level changes it by at most 1%. The offset comes from registering one contrast to another, not from noise.

The GE fixture is the exception. Its delta-M volumes are registered to the M0 through a 5 mm smoothed reference, and noise does make that worse: 0.92 mm with no noise, 1.70 mm at the calibrated level and 2.17 mm at ten times. The no-noise value alone is already the largest offset of any fixture.

Does the cost function matter?

No cost function removes it. Every MCFLIRT cost leaves an offset when one contrast is registered to a reference of another, and registering each volume type to its own template brings every cost below 0.10 mm. Among the control-label fixtures 3D GRASE, where label and control differ most, is the worst case for every cost; the GE fixture is worse still.

Blurred references bias registration

Production smooths the reference by 5 mm on the GE path (short series of five volumes or fewer), and every averaged template is somewhat blurred. To isolate that effect, each fixture volume was registered to a blurred copy of itself, so the true answer is the identity.

Registering a sharp image to a blurred one is biased. With a 5 mm isotropic kernel and mutual information, the error is 0.13 mm on 2D PCASL and 0.66 mm on the GE 3D fixture; across MCFLIRT costs on the GE fixture it ranges from 0.66 to 0.83 mm. Blurring both images by the same amount removes it, so the bias comes from the mismatch, not the blur itself. Removing the edge slices, padding or recentring the field of view leaves it between 0.54 and 0.70 mm on the GE fixture, so it is not an edge-slice effect.

Candidate screen on simulated motion

Each candidate ran on 23 simulated fixtures: four still-head acquisitions, aslscan's headmotion, and 18 motion fixtures generated for this probe with known trajectories. Each fixture was scored against an oracle: the same raw volumes moved back by their true poses and subtracted the same way.

The headline metric is the RMS difference between the candidate's mean delta-M and the oracle's, in the outer 0–3 mm of the brain, divided by the oracle's grey-matter delta-M. Edges are where misregistration shows: a 0.1 mm shift between control and label leaves a rim of raw tissue contrast, which is about a hundred times the perfusion signal. A value of 1 means the edge error is as large as the grey-matter signal.

Fixture by fixture

Production's error on still heads is small but not zero (median 0.07): that is the spurious label offset. Templates per volume type (C1, C2) remove it on still heads, but with real motion they lose to production, as the blur bias predicts: on mo_pcasl_drift C1 scores 0.35 against production's 0.12. Re-referencing production's transforms to one acquired volume (C3) changes nothing that matters. Sharing one transform per pair (C4) and zig-zag regression (C8) are perfect when there is no within-pair motion and fail badly when there is (next section).

Zig-zag regression

Wang (2012) removes the spurious label/control pattern after the fact: register every volume, then regress a +1/−1 alternation out of each motion parameter. ASLtbx and ExploreASL both do this. Candidate C8 is that method, with M0 volumes registered separately (Sudipto Dolui's suggestion in #266).

On still heads it is perfect: edge error 0.00 on pcasl1pld, against production's 0.13. It fails as soon as real motion has a component at the alternation frequency, and almost every real motion does.

Spikes alias onto the alternation

The headmotion trajectory has no alternation at all, but with ten pairs, a drift and three spikes, least squares still finds one in the true parameters: up to 0.15 mm and 0.23 deg. Subtracting it puts a zig-zag into every volume, and the edge error goes from 0.41 (joint estimation, no regression) to 6.84. A Huber fit down-weights the spikes (0.08 mm on the true parameters) and brings it back to 2.96; on mo_pcasl_spikes it goes from 5.83 to 0.34, production's level.

Drift is removed along with the zig-zag

A slow drift moves the head a little between the control and the label of every pair. That step is real, and it alternates in sign relative to the pair's mean, so the regression removes half of it from each volume. The pair error triples, and because edges are so sensitive, the edge error goes from 0.12 (production) to 1.76 on mo_pcasl_drift. Huber cannot help here: the drift is not an outlier. Pair-shared transforms (C4) fail the same way (1.74).

Registration engines and the short list

The plan's estimator screen runs every variant of each family on the 26 development fixtures and keeps the best of each by two metrics fixed in advance: relative-pose error (are the volumes aligned with each other?) and pair error (are control and label aligned?). C1 and C2 were crossed with four registration engines (MCFLIRT, AFNI 3dvolreg, ANTs rigid and niimath's port of 3dAllineate), the grouping (by type, or by type and delay), the common frame and the cross-type step. Each engine was first checked against known rigid transforms (all recover them to within 0.31 mm for 2.5–3.8 mm motions).

3dvolreg stands out on relative pose: 0.063 mm for C1 against production's 0.171 mm. Least squares within one contrast is the right cost for a same-contrast template. Its pair error is below production's (0.033 mm for C2 against 0.041 mm), but its edge error is not: 0.37 against 0.13. ANTs has the worst pair error of the engines (0.097 mm).

Which reference the later steps receive

The HMC target and the image handed to later steps need not be the same. D0 keeps production's reference and expresses the candidate's transforms in its frame; D1 rebuilds production's reference from the same volume selection after the candidate's correction; D2 hands on the candidate's own template. The relative metrics do not change between policies; what changes is how well the corrected volumes agree with the reference the later steps see.

Real data: production's baseline

Production's HMC ran on 281 runs from 25 datasets in the aslprep-zoo panel (the anatomy-free harness, which builds the reference, corrects motion and subtracts). Real data have no truth, so the probe uses indirect measures. Alternation power is the fraction of the estimated motion parameters' power at the label/control frequency: real head motion has little reason to alternate with labelling, so high values point to spurious label motion.

3D stack-of-spirals stands out (median 0.130, against 0.015 for the median of the other families). The simulations predict the most spurious label motion where label and control differ most, which is the case for 3D readouts. The 3D GRASE fixture explains its 1 mm coregistration error. Choosing the BBR sign from the reference's measured contrast, which the plan already calls for, may be the simpler fix.

Which real runs could use a perfusion image

A run is eligible for the perfusion reference only if its perfusion image has reproducible edges. The statistic correlates the gradient magnitude of two half-run perfusion images. On the 259 real runs with control-label pairs, stack-of-spirals runs are far above every other family (median 0.93); most families sit near the placeholder limit of 0.5, so its calibration will decide eligibility for them.

On real data there is no truth, only disagreement. Across 75 runs the perfusion registration lands a median 1.21 mm from the conventional one, and 56% of runs differ by more than 1 mm. Those runs are what the blind review has to adjudicate.

Saturation-recovery normalization (#673)

With a pre-saturation pulse and no background-suppression inversions, each control image is a point on a recovery curve, M0 (1 − e−t/T1eff), where t is the labeling duration plus the delay plus the slice time. Volumes at different delays therefore differ in contrast, and production registers them to one another as they are. C5 divides every control and label volume by its recovery factor and registers the normalized volumes jointly. It refuses runs it cannot model (3D readouts, inversion pulses, no slice timing, a single delay) and processes them as production. Its three fixtures are a 2D EPI pCASL protocol with five delays (0.2–2.2 s) and pre-saturation at labeling start, and two negative controls that C5 must refuse: the same protocol with two inversion pulses (BS), and with a 3D GRASE readout.

C5 refuses both negative controls and reproduces production on them exactly. On the eligible protocol, the voxelwise fit looks perfect on the still head and is the worst arm with motion. The error jumps at the delay-block boundaries and grows with the motion, which points to a feedback loop: the T1eff map has sharp edges, normalizing by it at the estimated pose imprints those edges there, and registration then confirms the pose it was given. With no motion, the identity is that fixed point. A single T1eff per slice, in the acquisition frame, cannot carry a pose. A joint contrast and motion model (C6)

C6 predicts every control and label volume in its own contrast and fits the poses jointly, with no cross-contrast cost and no fixed reference. As first written it is not identifiable, so the plan required an identifiability test before any prototype. The test is exact and local: the Fisher information at the truth, with every nuisance parameter (static image, contrast, perfusion, delay weights) eliminated, bounds the confounded motion patterns: a whole delay block moved, every label moved, and the labels of one delay moved. Criterion, fixed in advance: at most 0.1 mm at white noise of 2% of the brain control signal.

Free contrast maps absorb every displacement shared by a group, as the plan expected. A perfusion image that is free everywhere absorbs any shift shared by the labels on single-delay data, and label shifts proportional to the delay weights on multi-delay data without pre-saturation. The displaced label differs from the static image by about the shift times its gradient, which a free perfusion image can cancel at every delay at once. Fixing the perfusion image to zero outside a brain support makes every pattern identifiable, to 0.01 mm. The test passed, with the support as part of the model.

The prototype

The prototype (PyTorch on the GPU, fitted on the host because the ASLPrep image has no PyTorch) ran into two problems the local test cannot see. First, the model cannot reproduce how the simulator forms a moved image: with the poses frozen at the truth the fit stays above the noise, and free poses settle up to 0.15 mm away (from any start, so not a local minimum). An acquisition model (a finer in-plane grid, cropped in k-space, with a learned weight per phase-encoding line) halves that misfit but barely moves the poses. Second, with that misfit the fit preferred to leave labels unmoved and absorb their displacement into the perfusion image (pair error 0.54 mm on the synchronous fixture), because moving a volume costs the misfit while absorbing costs only the residual outside the support. Bounding the perfusion image to ±3% of the static image (delta-M is about 1% of the control signal) removes that.

The short list through full ASLPrep (Task 7)

Each short-listed estimator was wired into full ASLPrep by handing its HMC reference and transforms to a fresh run as precomputed derivatives. ASLPrep then skips its own HMC and builds everything after it from the candidate's products: M0 and fieldmap registration, the coregistration reference, the mask, coregistration and CBF. Production's harness output, injected the same way, reproduces the baseline run (motion, M0 and mask identical, coregistration within 0.002 mm). The CBF target is an oracle run given the true poses: the same quantification of the same data without motion-correction error, inside the true brain. It is built in each arm's own frame, so that neither map is resampled more than the other: production and C9 use production's reference, which with a separate M0 can sit 2.5 mm from the volumes, and are compared with an oracle built in that frame. (A first version compared every arm with an oracle in the static frame; resampling that oracle into production's frame smoothed it and showed production a 3% grey-matter bias that was not there.) Values are relative to the oracle's grey-matter CBF.

Getting there exposed an ASLPrep bug: precomputed transforms are silently dropped whenever atlases are enabled (the default), because the atlas datasets are collected after the user's and each replaces the transforms. The probe works around it; the fix belongs in ASLPrep.

Caveats and next steps

  • All candidate results are on development fixtures. The held-out fixtures have not been scored, so nothing here is final.
  • The simulator moves the head through a B0 field fixed to the scanner, so large spikes are not fully rigid-correctable even with the true poses; the scores are normalized by a median over pairs for that reason.
  • Edge sensitivity may be higher in simulation than in real data (no partial-volume blurring of the brain edge beyond the voxel grid). The real-data runs of the candidates will test whether the drift penalty on pair-based methods carries over.
  • Next: a failure guard for production (detect disagreement with c2-volreg or an implausible FD, then fall back); choosing the BBR sign from the reference's grey/white contrast; score the held-out fixtures; and the full-pipeline runs of the short list (Task 7).
