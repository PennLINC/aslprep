# Plan: head-motion-correction probe

Branch: `worktree-hmc-probe`. Status: revision 13, after six Codex adversarial reviews (log at
the end). Issues: #702,
#705, #707 (bugs); #508, #673, #700, #672 (scope). #703 is being fixed separately (branch
`m0scan-skip-regrid`) and is treated here as done.

## Goal

Choose the head-motion-correction (HMC) method ASLPrep should use, by testing alternatives to
the current one on simulated data with known truth and on real data. "Best" means:

1. it invents no motion in motion-free data;
2. it recovers real motion;
3. it keeps each control-label pair aligned, so that delta-M and CBF are preserved;
4. it leaves the series aligned with the reference that coregistration uses;
5. it is deterministic, fast enough, and maintainable.

The outcome may be one method or a small set of acquisition-specific ones.

## Current method

`aslprep/workflows/asl/hmc.py` splits the series by volume type and runs MCFLIRT
(`cost='mutualinfo'`) on each type. Every type is registered to one common reference, built by
`aslprep/workflows/asl/reference.py` (`RobustAverage` of the selected volumes). The selection
(`SelectHighestContrastVolumes` in `aslprep/interfaces/reference.py`) has two branches:

- GE processing (`use_ge`, which sets `prioritize_m0`): an included M0, then a separate M0, then
  the order below. The reference is also smoothed with `--smooth_kernel` (5 mm by default).
- otherwise: CBF, then delta-M, then an included M0, then a separate M0, then control.

The per-type parameters are then stitched back together.

This follows Wang et al. (2008) only in part: that paper realigns label and control series
separately, each to its own target, and reports no significant benefit from doing so in its
data. ASLPrep registers every type except the reference's own across contrasts.

### Probe results (2026-10-12)

A standalone replica of the HMC step, not the full pipeline: five motion-free fixtures were
regenerated with aslscan at three noise levels (none, the fixture's level, ten times the
variance), and MCFLIRT was run per volume type in the `pennlinc/aslprep:test` image. The score
is the RMS displacement over an 80 mm sphere, in mm; any nonzero value is error. Each noise
level has one realization.

The replica is simplified, and its tables are historical evidence, not the baseline:

- the reference is a plain mean, not `RobustAverage`, and the GE reference is not smoothed;
- the data are raw aslscan output, without the fixture export step that divides M0 by
  `m0_divisor` (this changes the intensity scale that least squares sees);
- the own-type rows stop after registering each type to its own mean, and that mean includes
  the volumes being scored. They say nothing about the second step of C1 and C2, which
  registers the type means to a common frame.

Values are rounded summaries of `results.json`, not upper bounds.

**The spurious motion is systematic, not noise.** Current settings, common sharp M0 reference:

| Fixture | No noise | Fixture noise | 10x noise | Scatter across volumes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pasl_multipld` | 0.07 | 0.07 | 0.07 | <= 0.002 |
| `pcasl1pld_grase` | 0.38 | 0.38 | 0.39 | 0.01 |
| `pcasl1pld` | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.001 |
| `pcasl_multipld` | 0.17 | 0.17 | 0.17 | 0.03 |
| `pcasl1pld_ge3d` (delta-M) | 0.93 | 1.70 | 2.17 | single volume |

Only the GE delta-M estimate gets worse with noise, and it is already wrong without any.

**The Gaussian-resampled M0 reference explains the sizes in #705 and #707.** Rebuilding the
reference with `antsApplyTransforms -n Gaussian -t identity`, as `reference.py` does for a
separate M0, gives 1.3 mm on `pasl_multipld` (issue: 1.2), 0.64 on `pcasl1pld_grase` (0.6) and
0.27 on `pcasl1pld` (0.3), and raises the `pasl_multipld` pair discrepancy from 0.003-0.005 mm
to a median of 0.11-0.14 mm. The resampling does not shift the image; it blurs it by about
0.8 voxel sigma. Why a blurred reference biases the fit is not known. This is #703.

**The reference matters; the cost function does not.** Worst common offset per fixture, at
fixture noise:

| Reference | Cost | `pasl_multipld` | `pcasl1pld_grase` | `pcasl1pld` | `pcasl_multipld` | `pcasl1pld_ge3d` delta-M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common (sharp M0) | mutualinfo | 0.07 | 0.38 | 0.05 | 0.17 | 1.70 |
| | normmi | 0.08 | 0.56 | 0.06 | 0.19 | 1.69 |
| | corratio | 0.11 | 1.15 | 0.06 | 0.26 | 2.50 |
| | normcorr | 0.07 | 1.43 | 0.06 | 0.23 | 3.05 |
| | leastsquares | 0.00 (suspect) | 1.80 | 0.05 | 0.52 | 2.31 |
| Own-type mean (within-type step only) | all five | 0.000-0.005 | 0.000-0.004 | 0.000-0.007 | 0.004-0.024 | not applicable |

- No cost function removes the bias of a cross-contrast reference. Mutual information is the
  best or close to the best of the five there (normalized MI and least squares each beat it
  once, by about 0.01 mm or less).
- Within a type, registering to the type's own mean leaves almost no common offset with any
  cost. Whether the composed transform to a common frame is also unbiased is untested.
- Multi-delay data keep 0.04-0.09 mm of scatter and up to 0.03-0.13 mm of pair discrepancy even
  with own-type references.
- `-stages 4`, `-smooth 0` and `-bins 64` do not help consistently.
- The single GE delta-M volume could not be registered to its M0 with any cost. This says
  nothing about registering repeated delta-M or CBF volumes to each other.

The probe scripts and data are outside the repository (`~/hmc-probe` in WSL). Task 1 rebuilds
them in the repository.

### Literature

| Approach | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Separate realignment of label and control | Wang et al. 2008 (ASLtbx) | The basis of the current method; no significant benefit in the paper's own data. |
| Regress the label/control pattern out of jointly estimated parameters | Wang 2012; ExploreASL | Does not say how to treat M0; would also remove real motion that happens to alternate. |
| Realign each type (and each PLD) to its own first volume | ASL-MRICloud | Close to own-type references. |
| MCFLIRT to the calibration image, re-referenced to an ASL volume | BASIL `oxford_asl` | Removes a common offset; does not address pair alignment. |
| Normalize static tissue, estimate, re-fit, estimate again | HCP-ASL (Kirk et al. 2025) | MCFLIRT to the calibration image around a saturation-recovery fit; this is #673. |
| Homogenize background-suppressed slices, register to M0, GLM subtraction | Suzuki et al. 2018 | For multiband 2D with background suppression. |
| Register each volume to a predicted image of its own contrast, iteratively | Suzuki et al. 2020 (ISMRM abstract 3868) | Gaussian process across PLDs; multi-delay with background suppression. |
| Groupwise registration with a PCA metric | renal and myocardial ASL | Mixed evidence: pairwise was more robust in myocardial ASL. |
| CNN affine registration of ASL series | NeuroImage 2023 | Compared with SPM only. |
| Contrast-agnostic learned registration | SynthMorph, EasyReg | Not tested on ASL volumes. |

No published head-to-head comparison of these on brain ASL was found. The ExploreASL and
HCP-ASL papers were not read in full: ExploreASL details come from summaries, and HCP-ASL
details from its code (`hcpasl/asl_correction.py`). Navigator-based, prospective and
reconstruction-level methods need acquisition changes and are out of scope.

## Scope

In scope:

- how the HMC reference is built, and what each volume is registered to;
- the registration engine and its settings;
- constraints between the two volumes of a pair;
- what to do with volume types that cannot be registered (delta-M, CBF);
- what coregistration is estimated on, as far as it interacts with HMC (#707);
- how every step that consumes the reference performs when a candidate changes it:
  skull-stripping, fieldmap-to-reference registration, M0-to-reference registration, and
  coregistration;
- multi-delay data (#508), saturation-recovery normalization for estimation only (#673), and
  estimation on gradient-corrected volumes (#700);
- a preliminary perfusion image as the coregistration reference, and an `auto` mode that
  chooses between it and the conventional reference per run (#672).

Out of scope:

- the #703 fix itself;
- the parts of #508 that are not about motion (brain masks, CBF bounds, report guidance);
- reporting voxels whose fit ends at a bound as failures in production (#705, direction 3).
  The probe measures that fraction, but the reporting change belongs to the CBF workflow;
- changes to quantified intensities (the `full` mode of #673, GLM subtraction);
- the side-by-side registration reportlets of #672 (the probe compares registrations
  numerically and by blind review; reportlets belong to the production change);
- artifact detection, slice-to-volume correction, and acquisition-level methods.

## Baseline

The baseline is the production workflow with the #703 fix applied, called as it is: the
harness uses ASLPrep's own reference selection, `RobustAverage`, the GE reference smoothing,
and the exported fixtures. Its numbers are measured in Tasks 1 and 3. The "common (sharp M0)"
rows above only suggest what to expect.

The baseline revision is pinned in Task 3 (at the time of writing, `m0scan-skip-regrid` at
`845402a`). That branch does more than skip the resampling:

- it has already removed the known failures for #705 on `pasl_multipld` and for #707 on
  `pasl_multipld` and `pcasl1pld_grase`, because they pass there;
- it also gives voxels with almost no M0 signal no CBF estimate, which changes fit-bound counts
  independently of HMC;
- it skips the Gaussian resampling only when the M0 is already on the ASL grid. A separate M0
  on a different grid is still blurred.

Success is therefore defined against what still fails at the pinned revision, not against the
sizes reported in the issues.

C0, C1, C2, C4, C5, C6 and C8 are estimators. C3 is a frame policy and D a downstream reference
policy: neither changes the relative motion estimates. What is evaluated is a combination of
one estimator, one frame policy and one downstream policy, and each policy is judged on the
outcome it targets (see the gates).

### Downstream reference policy

The HMC target and the image handed to later steps need not be the same. Production already
uses several images, and each policy must say what every consumer receives:

| Consumer | Image it receives now |
|---|---|
| M0 registration (`init_m0scan_hmc_wf`) | the raw HMC reference |
| Fieldmap registration (`init_coeff2epi_wf`) | the bias-corrected reference and its preliminary mask |
| Unwarping, then skull-stripping | the HMC reference, unwarped, then masked |
| Coregistration (`init_bold_reg_wf`) | the unwarped, bias-corrected reference |

The policies:

| ID | Policy |
|---|---|
| D0 | Unchanged: the production reference, exactly as built now, for every consumer. The candidate changes HMC only, and its transforms are expressed in that reference's frame |
| D1 | Rebuilt: the same volume selection as production, averaged after applying the candidate's HMC transforms. This is the former P2 |
| D2 | The candidate's own template (C1, C2, C6), for every consumer |
| D3 | D1 for every consumer except coregistration, which receives a preliminary perfusion image (#672) |
| DA | Automatic: coregistration is estimated from both the D1 reference and the D3 perfusion image, and one of the two transforms is kept per run by a rule fixed in advance (#672) |

Rules that hold for every policy:

- The replacement image enters where the HMC reference enters now: built from raw volumes with
  HMC transforms only, never after distortion correction, so that it is unwarped once.
- For each policy the plan's implementation note states, per consumer, the image, whether it is
  raw or corrected, its mask, and the transform that links its frame to the HMC frame. A
  separate M0 is registered to whatever image the policy gives the M0 consumer, and the note
  says whether that registration is within or across contrast.
- ASLPrep collects single-band references but does not support them for ASL (`fit.py`), so they
  are out of scope; the fixtures and the panel contain none.
- Production can select reference volumes that never get an HMC transform: a separate M0, or
  volumes that are dropped before HMC because another type takes processing priority (as with
  the first volume of `jsr-aslprep-debug`, mislabelled `cbf`). Volumes labelled `n/a` or `noRF`
  are dropped as well and are not HMC groups. D1 is defined only over volumes that are in the
  corrected series: where production's selection lies outside it, D1 falls back to D0 for that
  run, and the run is reported as such. The harness reproduces production's volume reduction
  exactly, and a test checks this on those three datasets.

### D3: the perfusion image (#672)

The perfusion image is built after HMC, from the candidate's corrected volumes, on the raw
grid and before distortion correction (so that it is unwarped once, like the other references).
The same builder runs in the harness and, in Task 7, in the workflow; it uses no truth and no
kinetic fit.

**Inputs by data type.**

- Control-label pairs: the pairwise differences. For multi-delay data, only the pairs at
  delays at or above the run's median post-labeling delay (from the sidecar, a fixed metadata
  rule; arrival time is not known before the kinetic fit). Each kept pair has equal weight.
- Repeated derived volumes (delta-M or CBF series): those volumes.
- A single derived volume (the GE product case): that volume.

**Levels** (fixed before results are seen):

| Level | Image |
|---|---|
| P-mean | Mean of the inputs |
| P-robust | Voxelwise median of the inputs (resistant to spike pairs); equal to P-mean for one input |
| P-winsor | P-robust with values above the 99th percentile inside the conventional brain mask clipped, to suppress macrovascular signal |
| P-masked | P-winsor, registered with the conventional brain mask as the moving-image weight |

P-masked uses FLIRT's input weighting (`-inweight`), which restricts the cost to the weighted
voxels in both the initialisation and the BBR stage. The mask is the one ASLPrep already makes
from the conventional (D1) reference before unwarping, on the same raw grid, so it does not
depend on D3. bbregister has no moving-image weight, so P-masked is a FLIRT-only level.

**Eligibility.** Separate rules by data type, each computed from the builder's inputs by the
same function in the harness and in the workflow; a missing or non-finite statistic makes a run
ineligible.

- Pairs: at least four pairs after delay selection, and an edge-content statistic at or above a
  limit. The statistic is the correlation, inside the conventional brain mask, between the
  perfusion image's gradient magnitude and that of a split-half replicate of itself: it asks
  whether the image has reproducible edges, not only reproducible values. Split-half
  correlation of the values themselves is reported alongside, as in the real-data metrics.
- Repeated derived volumes: the same statistic over halves of the derived series.
- A single derived volume: no replicate exists, so it cannot be judged by these rules; it forms
  its own arm ("D3-single") and is reported separately, never pooled.

The pair count and the limit are hypotheses, not settings. They are calibrated in Task 5b
against registration success (truth in simulation, adjudication on real development data),
and the classification's sensitivity to each is reported over a range (pairs 2-8, limit
0.3-0.7) before both are frozen. Runs near the limit (the motion screen already has some
between 0.45 and 0.55) are reported as a separate band.

**Contrast of the reference.** fMRIPrep's coregistration assumes a T2-like contrast (grey
matter brighter than white): `contrast_type='t2'` for bbregister and FLIRT's default signed BBR.
Image type alone does not guarantee it: M0 and control contrast depend on recovery and readout,
and perfusion boundaries on arrival and signal quality. The contrast that decides whether a
BBR sign suits a reference is measured without the registration under test:

- in simulation, from the truth: the phantom's tissue maps in the reference's frame;
- on real data, only on the adjudicated development subset, through the alignment the reviewers
  accepted.

It is measured as local contrast across the white-matter boundary (samples along the surface
normals, about 2 mm on each side), as well as a global grey/white ratio. Two backend-specific
variants are defined in advance: the default sign, and the opposite sign
(FLIRT `-bbrslope` reversed; bbregister `--t1` instead of `--t2`). The opposite sign is tested
for a family only if its measured contrast is inverted on development data. Ratios measured
through a candidate's own transform are reported as diagnostics only.

**Stress cases.** The probe phantom has tissue-defined perfusion with smooth modulation, and the
existing macrovascular recipe uses tissue-wide constants, so simulation alone cannot show what
clipping, masking and robust averaging are for. Task 4 adds targeted fixtures, each checked to
show its effect in the simulated images before it is used:

- focal macrovascular signal (a phantom variant with arterial blood-volume blobs along the
  major arteries, through aslscan's per-voxel `abv` and `aatt` maps);
- a region of absent perfusion (perfusion set to zero in one territory);
- subtraction artifacts (the within-pair motion fixtures);
- stack-of-spirals readout (ringing).

Until these exist, simulation is used to check D3's implementation and geometry only; no level
is eliminated on simulation alone.

### DA: automatic selection (#672)

DA estimates coregistration twice, from the D1 reference and from the D3 image, and keeps one
transform per run. The two optimized costs are not compared with each other: they come from
different source images with different contrast and noise, and the FLIRT path does not expose
its cost at all. Instead, each transform is evaluated on common evidence:

- both transforms are scored by the BBR cost of the D1 reference and by the BBR cost of the D3
  image, each measured with FLIRT's measure-cost schedule at a fixed transform (so each image
  ranks the two transforms on the same footing);
- a transform is preferred when both images rank it better; if they disagree, the run keeps D1
  and is flagged.

Further rules, fixed in advance:

1. If the D3 image is not eligible, keep D1.
2. If the two transforms differ by less than a fixed distance (RMS displacement in mm over the
   T1w brain mask, set in Task 3 from the adjudication uncertainty and the gate 4 margins, not in
   voxels), keep D1. This tie preference is itself checked: the reviewed subset includes ties.
3. If either registration fell back to its initialisation, or BBR was disabled, use the
   other's; if both did, keep D1. A missing cost counts as a disagreement (keep D1, flag).

**Validating DA.** The population is the eligible runs of the reviewed subset (development for
the rule, held-out for the decision) in which D1 and D3 differ by more than the tie distance and
the reviewers agree on which is better; ties and reviewer disagreements are excluded and
counted. The sampling unit is the subject (sessions of a subject together). DA is compared with
the two fixed policies, always-D1 and always-D3, not with a coin: it must beat the better of
the two by more than the bootstrap uncertainty (by subject). If fewer than 20 informative
disagreements exist, the result is "insufficient evidence" and DA is not adopted. DA is also
judged like any other policy (gate 4).

### Bounding the combinations

Crossing every factor is not feasible: C1 and C2 alone have 2 estimators x 4 engines x
2 groupings x 2 common frames x 2 cross-type methods = 64 variants, before the frame and
downstream policies. The screening is staged, and the lists are fixed before results are seen:

1. **Estimator screen (harness only, Task 5):** all estimator variants with D1 and no frame
   policy, on the development fixtures. The best variant of each estimator family by relative
   pose and pair error goes forward, plus C0: one slot per family (C1, C2, C4, C8 now; C5 and
   C6 each take their slot when Task 6 delivers them, and a family that is not delivered
   leaves its slot empty), so at most seven.
2. **Policy screen (harness, Task 5):** those with D0-D3 and C3, where the policy applies.
   D3's perfusion levels are not screened here: the harness has no coregistration. They are
   chosen in Task 5b.
3. **Full pipeline (Task 7):** at most four combinations plus C0, each with production and
   `--sloppy` settings.

One interaction test is kept outside the screen: each short-listed estimator is also run with
D0, in case D1 hides a difference between estimators.

C1 and C2 are always scored on the complete composed transform, including the cross-type step.
Their null tests include short series (two to four volumes per type) and a leave-one-out
template, so that a volume is not scored against a mean that contains it.

A group with one volume has no template. `pasl_multipld`, the fixture behind #705, has eight
delays with one label and one control each, so grouping by type and PLD leaves only such
groups. The grouping is therefore a factor with defined fallbacks:

- by type only (all PLDs of a type share a template);
- by type and PLD where a group has at least three volumes, falling back to by-type otherwise.

`pasl_multipld` is kept as the applicability test for the fallback, and a multi-delay recipe
with repeated volumes per PLD is added for template validation.

C5 is eligible only where #673 says the model holds: a known pre-saturation pulse and its
timing, a 2D readout, several delays, slice timing, and no inversion pulses. No existing recipe
enables pre-saturation, so Task 4 adds one eligible recipe and two negative controls
(inversion-suppressed, and 3D) on which C5 must be refused or shown to do no harm.

C1 and C2 are crossed with the registration engine: MCFLIRT, AFNI `3dvolreg`, ANTs rigid (as in
the closed PR #506), and niimath `-allineate`.

Engine notes:

- `3dvolreg` and ANTs are in the test image. niimath is not installed anywhere yet.
- niimath `-allineate` is a port of AFNI 3dAllineate (costs `ls`, `hel`, `nmi`, `lpc`, `lpa`)
  with a faster adaptive engine. It registers only the first volume of a 4D file, so it needs a
  loop over volumes. The fast engine rejects `-warp`; which `-warp` value is rigid has not been
  confirmed.

### C6: differentiable contrast model

Per run, fit jointly:

- a static-tissue image;
- a contrast map per volume type and per PLD (or a low-rank basis of such maps);
- a perfusion-weighted image;
- six rigid parameters per volume.

Each volume is predicted in its own contrast and compared with the data by least squares, so no
cross-contrast cost and no fixed reference are involved.

As stated, the model is not identifiable, and Task 6 starts by fixing that on paper:

- unrestricted contrast maps can absorb a displacement shared by all volumes of one type or
  PLD, so the maps must be restricted (for example, smooth scalings of the static image, or a
  basis with far fewer degrees of freedom than a displacement field needs);
- nothing anchors the global frame, so one pose, or the mean pose, is fixed;
- the written model is then tested for identifiability before any optimization work: motion
  confounded with PLD (every volume at one PLD displaced), motion confounded with type, and
  several initializations.

**The written model (2026-10-17).** Each control or label volume `v`, of delay `d`, is predicted
as `y_v = W_θv [ c_d · S − w_d · P · 1(label) ]`:

- `S`, a static-tissue image (free per voxel);
- `c_d = exp(Σ_k a_dk B_k)`, a contrast field per volume group, with `B_k` the ten polynomials
  up to order 2 (or a scalar gain per group);
- `P`, one perfusion image, free per voxel **inside a brain support and zero outside it**, with
  a scalar weight `w_d` per delay (the first fixed);
- six rigid parameters per volume, with the mean pose fixed.

**Identifiability test** (`scripts/hmc_probe/c6_identifiability.py`). The Fisher information at
the truth, with every nuisance parameter eliminated, gives the Cramér–Rao bound on the confounded
patterns: one delay block moved, every label moved, and the labels of one delay moved. The
criterion, fixed beforehand, was a bound of at most 0.1 mm / 0.1° at white noise of 2% of the
brain control signal. Fixtures: `c5_presat` (five delays with pre-saturation), `rc_pasl_multipld`
(eight delays, one pair each) and `rc_pcasl` (one delay).

- Free contrast maps: every group-shared displacement is absorbed (54 null directions on
  `c5_presat`), as stated above. The test detects it.
- A free perfusion map per delay: the delay-block patterns are absorbed.
- Smooth contrast or scalar gain with one perfusion map **without a support**: identifiable only
  on `c5_presat`. On single-delay data, any displacement shared by the labels is absorbed by
  `P`. On multi-delay data without pre-saturation, label displacements proportional to `w_d`
  are absorbed: shifting each label by `k·w_d` changes it by about `k·w_d·∇S`, which
  `δP = −k·∇S` cancels at every delay at once (0.4–0.9 mm at 2% noise on `rc_pasl_multipld`).
  Pre-saturation breaks this only because it makes the tissue contrast differ between delays.
- **With `P` fixed to zero outside a brain support: every pattern is identifiable on all three
  fixtures**, at most 0.010 mm / 0.012° at 2% noise and 0.025 mm / 0.030° at 5%. A support
  taken from the static image does as well as the true tissue mask.

The support constraint is therefore part of the model, not an option. The test is local and
assumes the model is correct and the noise white. It says nothing about local minima, about
misspecified contrast (partial volume, non-smooth contrast), or about structured physiological
noise. Those, and the several initializations the plan asks for, are for the prototype. The
bounds are far below the estimators' simulated errors (0.03–0.09 mm), which come from bias, not
noise (the fixtures are nearly noiseless). C6 can only help by removing the cross-contrast bias.

**Prototype (2026-10-17).** `scripts/hmc_probe/c6_model.py` (PyTorch, Adam over a smoothing
pyramid, on the GPU) is fitted on the host by `c6_fit.py`; the harness candidate `c6` reads the
fits, because the ASLPrep image has no PyTorch. On the fixtures:

- With the poses frozen at the truth, the loss does not reach the noise: the model cannot
  reproduce how aslscan forms a moved image (a fine-grid image resampled, then acquired in
  k-space with T2* decay along phase encoding). Free poses converge to the same place from zero
  or from the truth (no sign of local minima), up to 0.15 mm from the truth.
- An acquisition model (`hires`: maps on a grid twice as fine in-plane, k-space cropped, a learned
  weight per phase-encoding line) halves that floor but barely moves the poses.
- On the type-confounded fixtures the fit left the labels unmoved and absorbed their displacement
  into `P` (pair error 0.54 mm on `mo_pcasl_synchronous`, against production's 0.10), with
  either support. Moving a volume costs the interpolation misfit; absorbing its displacement
  costs only the residual outside the support. The local test is right, and the misspecified
  model prefers the wrong answer anyway.
- Bounding `P` to ±3% of the static image (`p_bound`; delta-M is about 1% of the control signal)
  removes that: pair error 0.04 mm on `mo_pcasl_synchronous` with the plain interpolation model
  (production 0.10, `c2-volreg` 0.13), and a 0.05 mm worst case on `mo_pcasl_spikes` with the
  acquisition model (production 0.23). On drift both forms are still worse than production.

**Decision (2026-10-17): C6 is not short-listed, and C7 is not built.** On the 18 development
motion fixtures with pairs, the median edge error is 0.78 (interpolation model) and 0.90
(acquisition model), against production's 0.22 and `c2-volreg`'s 0.44. C6 beats production on
6 and 5 of them, and on still heads its misfit adds motion that is not there. Its gains are
confined to motion confounded with the volume type or the pairs, where `c2-volreg` already does
as well or better. The limit is the misfit between the model and the data, not identifiability
or the optimizer, and real data would have its own misfit. The code stays in the probe for the
record.

C6 is dropped if that test fails. A prior on the within-pair pose
difference gives the regularized form of C4. The prototype is PyTorch on the GPU
(`grid_sample` plus an optimizer); the production form is a Gauss-Newton solver in Rust, with no
trained weights.

### C8: zig-zag regression

The approach of Wang (2012), used by ASLtbx and ExploreASL, with M0 volumes kept out of the
regression (as Sudipto Dolui suggested in #266):

1. **Joint estimation.** The control and label volumes, in acquisition order, are registered
   together to one reference: their mean (both contrasts contribute equally to it). Estimating
   both types against the same target is what lets their alternation show up as a pattern in
   the parameters, rather than as two separately biased series.
2. **Regression.** Each volume's rigid transform is expressed about the field-of-view centre as
   six parameters (rotation vector in degrees, translation in mm; not MCFLIRT's `.par` Euler
   angles, an adaptation that keeps rotation and translation separable). Each parameter is
   regressed on an intercept and alternation regressors, and only the fitted alternation is
   subtracted; the intercept and everything else (drift, spikes) are kept.
   - **pooled** (Wang's method, the default): one regressor, +1 for controls and -1 for labels
     (centred), over the whole series;
   - **per-delay** (an extension for multi-delay data): one such regressor per delay, non-zero
     only for that delay's pairs, with no per-delay intercept. With one pair at a delay, that
     delay's regressor absorbs the pair's whole difference and makes its two cleaned transforms
     identical, which is the strict pair constraint C4, not zig-zag regression. Per-delay is
     therefore used only when every delay has at least two pairs; otherwise the pooled design
     is used, and the run is reported as having fallen back.
3. **Correction.** The cleaned parameters are turned back into transforms and the volumes are
   resampled once.
4. **M0 volumes.** In-series M0 volumes are not part of the regression. They are registered as
   their own group to their own mean, and that mean to the reference, as in C1. A separate M0
   scan is handled as in production (registered to the reference in the workflow, not in the
   series).
5. **Derived volumes** follow policy P1.

Combinations. Screened (the estimator screen): the control-label-mean reference with mutual
information, crossed with the fit (ordinary least squares, Wang's; or Huber) and the regressors
(pooled or per-delay): four combinations. Diagnostic only, never short-listed: production's
reference (built by the c0 path itself: its selection, `RobustAverage`, M0 regridding and GE
smoothing), normalized correlation, joint estimation without regression, and the reference
implementations' convention (below). Each run keeps an
audit trail: the initial matrices, the parameters and their convention and centre, the design,
the coefficients and the removed component.

The Huber fit is a development-stage amendment, not part of the list fixed before results:
it was added after the first trial (2026-10-14), before any held-out data were scored, and the
C8 combinations above are frozen from revision 11 on. On `headmotion`, whose true trajectory has no alternation at all, ordinary least
squares finds an alternation of up to 0.15 mm and 0.23 degree in the true parameters (from
its three 2 mm spikes and its drift, with only ten pairs) and subtracting it adds a spurious
zig-zag: C8's delta-M error in the outer 0-3 mm was 6.8 times the grey-matter mean, against 0.41
with the same joint estimation and no regression, and 0.31 for production. A Huber fit roughly
halves the aliased amplitude on the true parameters. The `spikes` fixtures are therefore the
second case, after `synchronous`, that C8 is expected to fail, and are reported separately.

What C8 is expected to get wrong, and how the plan tests it:

- **Real motion that alternates with label and control** is indistinguishable from the spurious
  pattern and is removed. The `synchronous` motion fixtures (labels displaced by 0.4 mm and 0.4
  degree) are the case it should fail; the size of its failure there is reported, not hidden in
  an average.
- **Within-pair motion** is partly alternating, so C8 is also scored separately on the
  `withinpairs` fixtures.
- **Few pairs:** with two to four pairs the regression has little to average over, and even a
  linear drift aliases onto the pooled regressor (a ramp of 0, 1, 2, 3 over two pairs loses
  0.5 to the alternation); the short-series fixtures cover this.
- **One pair per delay:** per-delay regression falls back to pooled there (above), so
  `pasl_multipld`-style runs never test per-delay regression; `paslrep` (three pairs per delay)
  does.
- **The real-data alternation-power diagnostic** is zero for C8 by construction, so it cannot be
  used to judge C8; C8 is judged on real data only by the decision metrics and the blind review.

**Comparison with ASLtbx and ExploreASL.** Both use the same parameter-cleaning function
(`cleanandsave_parameters`, from Ze Wang's `spm_realign_asl.m`; ExploreASL's copy is in its
modified `spm_realign.m`, called from `xASL_wrp_RealignASL.m`). For each volume `j` it takes
SPM's six parameters of `V(j).mat / V(1).mat` (translation in mm and Euler angles about the
world origin, relative to the first volume), projects a positional +/-1 regressor out of each
parameter with no intercept, re-references every parameter to the first volume, and rebuilds
`V(j).mat = spm_matrix(clQ(j,:)) * V(1).mat`. They differ from C8 in:

| | ASLtbx | ExploreASL | C8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Estimation | SPM realign (least squares, 5 mm smoothing, 2nd-degree B-spline), to the first volume | SPM realign (trilinear), two passes, to the mean | MCFLIRT, mutual information, to the control-label mean |
| Volumes regressed | the whole file | the whole file (M0 is normally a separate file) | control and label from `aslcontext.tsv`; M0 separate; P1 |
| Alternation | by position, label first unless `ctrfirst` | by position, label first | from `aslcontext.tsv` |
| Parameters | Euler angles about the world origin, relative to volume 1 | the same | rotation vector about the field-of-view centre |
| Regression | no intercept, then re-referenced to volume 1 | the same | intercept and centred regressor(s) |
| Multi-delay | single delay | zig-zag disabled for multi-PLD and multi-TE | pooled, or per-delay with at least two pairs per delay |
| Spikes | not handled | ENABLE spike removal, after the regression | OLS (Huber variant) |

For an even number of strictly alternating volumes the no-intercept projection equals C8's
centred regression, up to the re-referencing; the parameterization does not. With rotations
about a distant world origin, a rotation alternation appears in the translations too, and
subtracting Euler angles is not the same as subtracting rotation vectors. The diagnostic arm
`c8-spm` (`--opt convention=spm`) reproduces their convention and regression exactly on top of
C8's joint estimation, so that the remaining difference from the published tools is the
estimation engine and reference. Running SPM itself (MATLAB is available; SPM12 is not
installed) is not part of the plan.

### C7: learned network

Trained on aslscan simulations with randomized contrast, background suppression, PLD, SNR and
motion, conditioned on volume type and PLD. Risks: the gap between simulation and real data,
the effects aslscan does not model (below), and a null-test target of 0.05 mm, which is below
typical network accuracy. All current phantoms share one MNI anatomy (`tfmni-flat` changes
tissue values only), so C7 also needs independent anatomies for training, development and
testing; without them its results are limited to that anatomy. It is built only if C6 meets
the null and accuracy thresholds on the development simulations and misses the run-time budget.
That decision is made after Task 7 and uses no held-out data.

## Simulation

### What aslscan provides

- Rigid motion per volume from a trajectory file, random impulses, or linear drift.
- Within-volume motion: per-shot jumps and dropout for multiband 2D and segmented 3D readouts.
- Background suppression with arbitrary pulse times, including a pulse list per PLD.
- PCASL and PASL, single and multi-delay, 2D EPI with multiband, 3D GRASE, stack-of-spirals.
- Thermal noise, physiological fluctuations, B0 distortion.
- Truth: the poses, the within-volume events, the moved and the unmoved delta-M.

### What it does not model

- Motion resamples finished images: no spin-history or labeling-efficiency effects.
- The fieldmap stays in scanner space and does not change with head position.
- Through-plane motion carries slice timing with the anatomy.
- A separate M0 scan does not move, and is written on the acquisition grid at the identity.
- No fieldmap acquisition is written: a phantom B0 map distorts the images, but the output has
  no `fmap/` files, so ASLPrep runs no fieldmap registration on any current fixture.
- Gradient nonlinearity, so #700 cannot be tested with aslscan.
- Extracerebral tissue: the ASL phantom has no scalp or skull, and M0 falls to zero at the brain
  edge. Skull-stripping is therefore trivial in simulation and cannot be judged there.
- Moved volumes are trilinearly resampled and so slightly smoother than unmoved ones.

Real data therefore carry more weight than in a simulator-only study.

### Design

Factors, staged rather than fully crossed:

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | 2D PCASL single-delay; 2D PCASL multi-delay; 2D PASL multi-delay; multiband 2D with background suppression; background-suppressed 3D GRASE; GE-style delta-M with M0 |
| Motion | none; a constant nonzero pose on every volume (see below); drift; spikes; through-plane; between pairs only; within pairs; synchronous with label/control; confounded with PLD |
| Background suppression | off; weak; strong; varying with PLD |
| Noise | none; delta-M SNR 50, 8 and 5 per pair; three seeds each |
| M0 | separate on the ASL grid; separate on a different grid (resolution or orientation), with a known pose; included; absent |
| Derived volumes | single delta-M with M0; repeated delta-M series (aslscan does not write CBF volumes) |
| Series length | two to four volumes per type; the fixture's own length |

New recipes are needed for the motion versions of `pasl_multipld`, `pcasl_multipld`,
`pcasl1pld_grase` and `pcasl1pld_ge3d`, and for the background-suppression sweep. They live with
the probe, not under `aslprep/tests/data/aslscan/`, so that they do not enter the CI fixture
digest.

**Constant-pose control.** A separate M0 does not move in aslscan, so a constant pose on the
ASL series is a real ASL-to-M0 displacement, not an interpolation-only effect. The control
therefore has two forms, both scored as residual error against the known transform:

- included M0, which moves with the series: the correct estimate is the identity between
  volumes;
- separate M0: the correct estimate is the applied pose.

A cross-contrast bias exists without any resampling, so an offset in the constant-pose run is
not by itself an interpolation effect. Only the change between the matched zero-pose and
constant-pose runs is attributed to the added interpolation.

**Development and held-out sets** differ in trajectories, seeds and at least one acquisition
configuration. They share one anatomy, so held-out results do not show generalization across
anatomy; that rests on real data. Thresholds are fixed before the held-out set is scored.

### Metrics

1. **Relative pose:** per-volume RMS displacement error over the brain, relative to the first
   volume (median and worst volume). A change of frame cancels out of this by construction.
2. **Reference consistency:** the common offset between the corrected series and the reference
   that coregistration uses.
3. **Pair:** error in the relative control-label transform. The target is the known relative
   motion, which is zero only in the motion-free conditions.
4. **Delta-M:** signed bias and RMSE against an acquisition-aware target, for the interior and
   for the outer 3-6 mm shell separately. aslscan's delta-M truth excludes background-suppression
   attenuation and within-volume events, and moving then averaging is not the same as resampling
   the acquisition-grid truth. The target is therefore the delta-M reconstructed from the same
   data with the true (oracle) transforms, in a stated frame and coverage mask. The oracle's own
   error against the simulator truth is reported as the floor for each acquisition.
5. **CBF and ATT (target defined below):** bias by tissue, the 95th percentile of absolute error, and the fraction of
   brain voxels at a fit bound.
6. **End to end:** coregistration error composed with the HMC transforms.
7. **Confounds:** estimated against true framewise displacement.
8. **Cost:** run time, failures, and coverage lost to motion.

Metrics 1-4 and 7 come from the harness. Metrics 5 and 6 need full ASLPrep runs with the
candidate wired in (Task 7).

The target for metric 5 is the CBF and ATT that the same quantification gives from the
oracle-transform series, with the same model, calibration, frame and masks. aslscan and
ASLPrep's single-delay equation use different kinetic models, so correctly processed data
already differ from the physical truth, and spatial mixing can move a result towards that
truth. Error against physical truth is reported separately, with the oracle's own error as
the floor.

The existing scorer cannot be used as it is: `_hmc_deltam` compares preprocessed delta-M with
the raw series, so doing nothing scores perfectly; native CBF is scored on the phantom grid, so
a correct series in a different frame is penalized; and `score_frames` needs a coregistration
transform. Task 2 builds and validates a scorer for this benchmark.

### Steps that consume the reference

A candidate can change the reference in three ways: its contrast (M0, control mean, an own-type
or iterative template, a single acquired volume, a perfusion image), its sharpness and noise (how
many volumes are averaged, and after how many resamplings), and its frame. In
`aslprep/workflows/asl/fit.py` the reference then feeds:

| Consumer | Code | What a different reference can break |
|---|---|---|
| Fieldmap registration | `init_coeff2epi_wf` | The fieldmap is aligned to the reference; a different contrast or frame changes that fit and so the unwarping |
| Skull-stripping | `init_synthstrip_aslref_wf` (N4, then SynthStrip; `MockSynthStrip` under `--sloppy`), or `init_skullstrip_bold_wf` after unwarping | The ASL brain mask |
| Coregistration | `init_bold_reg_wf` (bbregister or FLIRT with BBR, initialised per `asl2anat_init`) | Boundary-based registration relies on grey-white contrast of a given sign; M0, control, background-suppressed control and perfusion images differ in both strength and sign |
| M0 registration | `init_m0scan_hmc_wf` | A separate M0 is registered to the reference; a non-M0 reference makes this cross-contrast |

The mask and the coregistration then reach further steps:

- The primary CBF fit does not use the ASL mask (`ComputeCBF` fits every voxel), so a worse
  mask leaves the main CBF map unchanged.
- SCORE and SCRUB, BASIL, the tissue-probability sampling, DVARS and CompCor, the QC metrics and
  the parcellation do use the mask, the anatomical transform, or both.

Every combination that changes the reference is scored on all of these, in addition to the HMC
metrics. The existing tests cover them only in part: `mask_coverage` checks that the mask
contains the brain but not that it excludes everything else, and the coregistration bounds were
set under `--sloppy`.

**Frames.** A candidate's reference is not at a known pose: a template or a corrected mean sits
wherever the estimator put it. A transform to T1w that compensates for a displaced reference is
correct, and comparing it with the anatomical pose alone would call it an error. Each metric
below is therefore computed in the scanner frame of the acquired volumes, through the full
chain. The scorer is validated by shifting a reference by a known amount and checking that the
scores of a correct chain do not change. A template built from imperfectly aligned volumes has
no single rigid pose; for those, the comparison is with the reference built from the oracle
transforms, and that limit is reported.

**Evaluation support.** A candidate that erodes its mask can improve any statistic computed
inside that mask. All cross-candidate CBF and QC comparisons therefore use one fixed support
per dataset (the truth-derived brain in simulation; the T1w-derived brain on real data, see
below), and the tissue each candidate's mask excludes is reported separately.

Simulation metrics:

9. **Brain mask:** Dice against the true brain mask, and the missed and the extra volume
   separately, each split into the outer shell and the rest. Weak evidence, because the phantom
   has no extracerebral tissue.
10. **Coregistration:** error of the composed map from each acquired volume to T1w against the
    known anatomical pose and the known volume pose (rotation as arc length at 70 mm, and RMS
    displacement), and the part of it that remains when the HMC transforms are replaced by the
    oracle's. Run with FLIRT-BBR under production and `--sloppy` settings, for each
    initialisation, on fixtures with a known T1w offset. bbregister needs FreeSurfer surfaces
    for the phantom (open decision 9); without them it is evaluated on real data only. Whether
    BBR was rejected in favour of its initialisation is recorded per run as a diagnostic: the
    rejection test compares BBR with the initialisation, not with the truth, and it is switched
    off for header initialisation and forced BBR. For D3 and DA, metric 10 is reported for each
    perfusion-image level and BBR sign variant, and for DA also its choice and the reason.
11. **Fieldmap and M0 registration:** error of the fieldmap-to-reference and M0-to-reference
    maps, in the same frame convention, on fixtures in which the fieldmap and the separate M0
    sit at the identity and at a known non-identity pose (Task 3a).
12. **Mask-dependent outputs:** on the fixed support, coverage of the parcellation (regions
    with a value), alignment of the tissue maps sampled into ASL space, and the change in
    SCORE and SCRUB outputs, on one configuration per acquisition family.

Metric 6 remains the end-to-end figure; metric 10 says how much of it is coregistration.

Real-data evaluation of these steps is in the next section.

## Real data

No truth exists, so three designs are combined:

1. **Low-motion and repeat scans:** false motion and repeatability.
2. **Semi-synthetic:** known rigid motion applied to low-motion runs. This shares aslscan's
   limits (no spin history), so it tests contrast and noise realism only.
3. **Native:** proxy metrics on data as acquired, computed on the fixed support.

Decision metrics, each with a direction fixed in advance and checked first on simulations and
semi-synthetic data with injected errors:

- split-half CBF reproducibility, with the halves balanced within delay, labeling duration and
  condition. A run with fewer than two pairs per delay is not eligible (odd and even pairs of
  `grotzinger_menopausal_asl` would hold different delays); such runs are judged on transform
  and alignment metrics only;
- test-retest ICC, only on a cohort of subjects with comparable repeat scans (see the panel);
  one repeatedly scanned subject does not give an ICC (both are repeatability, not bias);
- recovery of injected motion in the semi-synthetic runs;
- the fraction of brain voxels at a fit bound, and of negative grey-matter voxels;
- the energy of the mean delta-M (lower is better): the mean, within one mask shared by every
  candidate of a run (the union of their estimated brain masks), of the squared mean delta-M,
  compared between candidates of the same run only. Perfusion is common to every candidate of a
  run, so differences are driven by misregistration, apart from a perfusion-artifact cross term
  and the mask. On the development fixtures it ranks candidates as the truth-based edge error
  does (median Spearman 0.90 with the shared mask, 0.70 with each run's own mask; `ssd_check.py`),
  but not at still-head offsets: on the still and constant-pose fixtures the correlation is
  about zero or negative and production is ranked near the top while the truth ranks it near
  the bottom. It is therefore evidence about motion-driven errors only, and is not used to judge
  the spurious label offset. Pair-to-pair variance of delta-M was rejected: it rewards removing
  real within-pair motion (negative correlation with truth on the drift fixtures).

Diagnostics, reported with no assumed good direction:

- power at the label/control frequency in the motion parameters, and their correlation with
  PLD. Real motion can alternate with label/control, so less power is not necessarily better;
- correlation of absolute delta-M with the control-image gradient magnitude. Real perfusion
  edges lie on anatomical edges, so blurring lowers this without improving anything;
- delta-M temporal SNR, QEI, the number of volumes SCORE rejects, and, on the extended cohorts,
  the across-subject relationship between motion and CBF.

Reference-consumer metrics. Real data carry these, because the phantom cannot test
skull-stripping and has idealised contrast. No output of the baseline is used as truth: the
baseline agrees with itself perfectly, so such a standard could never be beaten.

Decision metrics, on a reviewed subset of runs spread over the acquisition families:

- **Adjudicated alignment.** Reviewers, blind to the combination, rate the ASL-to-T1w alignment
  of each run (and mark landmarks where the contrast allows), using images in a common physical
  frame. Two reviewers, with their disagreement reported as the uncertainty.
- **Adjudicated masks.** The same for the brain mask: acceptable, or failed with the kind of
  failure.
- **M0 alignment**, for runs with a separate M0: the M0 carried to T1w through the candidate's
  chain, rated in the same way, plus the regional change in calibrated CBF.
- **Residual distortion**, for runs with fieldmaps: alignment of the unwarped reference with
  the T1w along the phase-encoding axis, rated in the same way.
- **Test-retest agreement** of the masks, and of anatomical alignment after accounting for the
  head's position in each acquisition (the composed transforms themselves differ whenever the
  subject is repositioned), on comparable repeat pairs only.

Diagnostics, with no assumed good direction:

- agreement of each candidate's mask and transform with the baseline's, and with the T1w mask
  carried through each candidate's own chain;
- boundary contrast across the white-matter surface, which BBR itself optimises;
- the BBR rejection rate, stratified by initialisation;
- the grey-white CBF ratio in T1w space, which also depends on calibration, partial volume and
  physiology;
- differences between candidates in the fieldmap transform and in the unwarped reference.

Stack-of-spirals data are in the panel for this purpose: single volumes ring, so a reference
built from few volumes can fail these steps even when HMC succeeds.

A low-motion scan is not a motion-free scan, so no real-data null test is used as a gate.
Smoother maps or higher temporal SNR alone do not count as better accuracy. Results are
stratified by acquisition family and motion level. The panel is described next.

Rules for these:

- Nothing under the zoo is changed. Adapted copies are made elsewhere, each with a note of what
  was changed and why.
- The originals are kept as malformed-input tests: a candidate must fail on them in the same
  way the baseline does, or better, and never silently.
- Non-finite or degenerate outputs are rejected before any decision metric is computed.
- CBF-level metrics are not computed where the calibration is known to be wrong (`ds007522`
  sub-HML0202, `ds005752`, `ds004856` waves 2-3, `ds000240`). Transform, mask and alignment
  metrics do not depend on it.
- Anatomical inputs are fixed per dataset before any candidate is run, because the fixed
  evaluation support comes from them: `--skull-strip-t1w skip` for `ds008020`, and a filter for
  `shanmugan_hup6`, which has four T1w files (two runs, each raw and RMS). The resulting
  anatomical reference is checked by eye once.

#### What the panel provides

- **Comparable repeats** (same protocol, same condition, no intervention between):
  `ds007116` (three sessions), `ds004146` (two sessions), `ds005752` (two sessions),
  `jsr-aslprep-debug` (sessions 1 and 2), and the two resting runs of `ds003823` only if they
  fall in one session. The interval of each pair is recorded.
- **Robustness comparisons**, with no assumption that more agreement is better: `kashyap`
  (three receive coils), `ds004873` (four task conditions), `ds003823` (before and after a
  five-week intervention, and a rest run against a training run), `ds000234` (two readouts),
  `ds008020` (labeling durations and gradients), `ds004856` (waves years apart).
- **Separate M0.** Thirteen datasets. Every separate M0 has the same matrix and the same
  physical sampling as its ASL run. In `kashyap` the two are stored with opposite first-axis
  orientation (ASL as LAS, M0 as RAS), so their affines differ although the grids coincide;
  whether the #703 fix treats that as "on the grid" is checked in Task 0, and reported to the
  #703 work if it does not. `kashyap` is not a test of a blurred reference, because its
  reference comes from the in-series `m0scan`. `kashyap` and session 3 of `jsr-aslprep-debug`
  have two separate M0 candidates per run; the one used is pinned by a filter.
- **Fieldmaps that target ASL.** `2d_mb_pcasl` (pepolar), and `ds007522` (pepolar and
  phase-difference, acquired for BOLD with a different geometry). ASLPrep uses only the first
  estimator it finds, so the one actually selected is recorded and the other is run separately.
- **Semi-synthetic motion.** Long, still runs chosen in Task 0 from the development data only.
  The runs used to validate the direction of a metric are never the runs used for the final
  evaluation.
- **Edge cases the candidates must handle:** unpaired volumes, a first volume of another type,
  an in-series `m0scan` with `M0Type` `Separate`, and four-pair series.

No amount of extension below changes these, and no result is claimed for them from real data:

- **2D multi-delay PASL**, the acquisition behind #705. Simulation only.
- **Background-suppressed multiband multi-delay data**, the acquisition of Suzuki et al.
  `2d_mb_pcasl` declares no suppression.
- **A second 2D multiband multi-delay dataset.** `2d_mb_pcasl` is not from a public cohort.
- **Known motion.** No run has intentional motion or external tracking.
- **Repeated delta-M series.** `ds005752` has single derived volumes.
- **A separate M0 on a different physical grid** from its ASL run, so the Gaussian resampling

- **Fieldmaps for most families**, so residual distortion is judged on two datasets.

#### Protocols for the simulations

aslscan reads BIDS sidecars, but none of the panel protocols it would be most useful to copy
can be used as it is:

- `2d_mb_pcasl`: aslscan rejects `noRF` volumes;
- `kashyap`: it rejects an in-series `m0scan` with a separate M0;
- `grotzinger_menopausal_asl` and `lsa-aslprep-debug`: background suppression is on but the
  pulse timings are not in the sidecars;
- the 3D runs lack `PulseSequenceType`, a separate M0 needs its timing from an overlay, and 2D
  slice counts must fit the phantom.

Task 4 therefore adapts them: each recipe records its source run, what was removed, and every
value that had to be assumed. A recipe with assumed suppression or readout settings is a
sensitivity experiment on that family, not a match to the real protocol, and is named so.

#700 runs here as its own experiment with its own criterion, following that issue:

- simulation: known motion of 0-5 mm and 0-3 degrees applied to a motion-free reference, with
  the head at isocenter and 20-40 mm off it along z, distorted with a real or synthetic
  coefficient file; estimation on distorted and on corrected volumes; error in translation,
  rotation and worst-case displacement over the brain;
- the separate-M0-to-ASL registration under the same two strategies;
- real data with low and high motion, ideally off isocenter: differences in motion parameters,
  residual misalignment, and regional CBF;
- adoption criterion from the issue: not worth it if the estimation bias stays below about
  0.1 mm and 0.1 degree at the high end of realistic motion and off isocenter, and the median
  absolute regional CBF difference is under about 1 % with no spatial pattern.

A gradient coefficient file is available for `ds006131` (Siemens Prisma, stack-of-spirals), so
the real-data part runs on that dataset: on subjects chosen from its 51 by the motion screen,
since its runs have only four pairs each. A coefficient file is used only for the scanner it
belongs to; other datasets are added only if they are shown to come from the same scanner.

The experiment is conditional. It needs the coefficient file and subjects with enough motion;
if either is missing it is reported as not evaluated. It is an optional feature on top of
whichever HMC method is chosen, so it neither decides nor delays the HMC gates.

## Decision gates

Applied in order; a candidate must pass each one.

The gates apply to combinations (estimator, frame policy, downstream policy), and each
outcome is judged separately: relative alignment (metrics 1 and 3), reference consistency
(metric 2), end-to-end accuracy (metrics 4-6), and the reference consumers (metrics 9-12).

1. **Null:** on motion-free simulations, relative-pose error and pair discrepancy below a
   threshold, and end-to-end displacement below a second threshold. Both are set in Task 3 from
   the oracle floor and the noise-realization spread; 0.05 mm is the starting guess. A common
   offset that the downstream policy removes does not fail this gate.
2. **Accuracy:** better than C0 in the outcome the combination targets, and not worse in the
   other two, on `headmotion` and the new motion fixtures.
3. **Generalization:** no regression in any acquisition family on held-out simulations, and no
   regression in the real-data decision metrics, by criteria written down before the real data
   are scored.
4. **Reference consumers:** for a combination whose downstream policy is not D0:
   - simulations: metrics 10-12 within a margin of the baseline, and within an absolute limit;
   - real data: the adjudicated alignment, mask, M0 and distortion ratings not worse than the
     baseline's by more than a margin in any acquisition family, and above an absolute
     acceptability level, so that matching a poor baseline is not enough.

   The margins, the absolute limits and the size of the reviewed subset are written down in
   Task 3, before any candidate is scored. A combination with D0 passes this gate by
   construction and is judged on the other gates.
5. **Operation:** deterministic, within a run-time budget set in Task 3, and no new heavy
   dependency without agreement.

Among candidates that pass, the simplest is chosen. Trade-offs are reported per metric, not
folded into one score.

## Implementation rules

- Estimate on whatever images register best; quantify from the original intensities.
- Compose transforms and resample each volume once.
- Take pairing from `aslcontext.tsv`, never from odd and even volumes.
- Record, per volume, what it was registered to, with which engine and cost, and any identity
  fallback.
- Report the common offset and the within-pair difference separately.

## Conventions

- Local commands run in WSL in the `aslprep` micromamba environment; pytest is
  `python -m pytest`.
- MCFLIRT, ANTs and AFNI runs use the `pennlinc/aslprep:test` image.
- PyTorch work runs on the GPU (RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, 16 GB, visible from WSL).
- Probe data stay outside the repository. The real-data panel is read in place and never
  modified; outputs and working directories go elsewhere.
