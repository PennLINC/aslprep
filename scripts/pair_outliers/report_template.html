Multi-Delay Pair Exclusion

Can a kinetic model fit tell which control-label pairs of a multi-delay ASL run are corrupted? This study builds a model-based detector for ASLPrep's general kinetic model fit and tests it against a model-free per-delay rule, on $n_runs simulated runs with known corruption ($n_seeds noise realizations per condition) and on real runs with injected artifacts.

The method

ASLPrep fits the general kinetic model (CBF, ATT, aBAT, aBV) in every voxel, after averaging the pairs that share a delay. A corrupted pair biases that average, and with few pairs per delay one bad pair can move CBF and ATT. The detector asks how well each pair is predicted by everything else in the run, and flags pairs that are predicted much worse than the rest.

  1. Robust first fit. Fit the model to the voxelwise median of the pairs at each delay, so that a corrupted pair cannot distort the first prediction.
  2. Predict every pair. The prediction for pair p at delay d is the model's signal, plus a correction toward the other pairs at the same delay:
pred_p = model_d + w · median_{q ≠ p, q at d}(ΔM_q − model_d), w = m / (m + κ)

Here m is the number of other kept pairs at the delay and κ (the shrinkage) is 2. With many repeats the prediction leans on the pair's own delay, which absorbs systematic model misfit that would otherwise make a whole delay look corrupted. The correction is used only when at least three other pairs share the delay (w = 0 otherwise), because the median of one or two pairs is not robust: a corrupted neighbor would move it and spread its artifact to the clean pairs. With few repeats the prediction is the model's, and so comes from the pairs at the other delays. κ = 0 gives a same-delay median predictor and κ = ∞ the model alone; both are tested.

  1. Score each pair by three statistics of its residual over the brain: the mean squared residual standardized by each voxel's robust noise level (energy); the same after Gaussian smoothing (smoothed energy, which favors spatially coherent artifacts such as motion edges and slice dropouts over thermal noise); and the amplitude, the slope of a regression of the residual on the predicted signal plus a constant, which is −1 when a pair has lost all of its perfusion signal (failed labeling) and is not confused by global offsets.
  2. Flag outliers among the pairs with robust z-scores (median and MAD across all pairs) above 3.5, together with an effect-size floor (energy at least 1.25 times the median; amplitude at least 0.25 from the median), so that near-identical pairs are not split by tiny differences. At least one pair is kept at every delay.
  3. Refit without the flagged pairs and repeat until the set of flagged pairs stops changing (at most five passes). A pair flagged against a poor early fit can be restored later. The final fit is the ordinary ASLPrep fit on the kept pairs.

The code is aslprep/utils/pair_outliers.py (detect_outlier_pairs_multipld), with unit tests in aslprep/tests/test_utils_pair_outliers.py. It reuses the model and fitter in aslprep/utils/cbf.py and is not yet wired into the workflow.

Prior work

A literature search found no published method that rejects multi-delay ASL pairs by their residuals from a kinetic-model fit. Existing ASL rejection methods either work on single-delay CBF time series or compare the repeats of one delay without a model. The closest templates are in diffusion MRI, where RESTORE and FSL eddy's outlier replacement predict each measurement from a model and reject large standardized residuals.

This design departs from BASIL's Bayesian treatment. BASIL would handle a corrupted pair, if at all, through its noise model, and it has no heavy-tailed or mixture noise option. Rejection is explicit and auditable instead: each run's excluded pairs can be listed with their scores (as PennLINC/aslprep#683 proposes), and the final fit is unchanged ASLPrep code on the kept pairs. A robust likelihood inside a Bayesian fit is a reasonable alternative that this study does not test. The model-free comparator below is the per-delay rule that a parallel study (asl-outlier-eval) attributes to Harteveld et al. (MAGMA 2022): within each delay with at least three pairs, reject a pair if more than 20% of brain voxels lie more than 1.5 SD from that voxel's mean over the delay's repeats. I could not confirm that citation. With n repeats, no value can lie more than (n − 1)/√n standard deviations from the mean of the n, which is exactly 1.5 for four repeats. So this rule can never reject a pair at a delay with four or fewer repeats, and it needs about six to reject reliably. A fairer model-free baseline compares each pair with the mean and SD of the other pairs at its delay. With two or three other pairs that ratio has very heavy tails (a t distribution), so the 1.5 SD threshold would reject almost every clean pair. The leave-one-out version tested here therefore uses the t quantile that clean voxels exceed as often as they exceed 1.5 SD in a large sample (13.4%), with the same 20% rule. It was added after an independent code review pointed out that the published rule cannot act on these protocols.

Simulation

Each simulated run uses the MNI152NLin2009cAsym tissue maps at 4 mm, with smooth spatial variation in CBF (GM median about 53 mL/100 g/min in the noise-free fit), ATT (longer in white matter and toward the back of the brain) and sparse macrovascular signal. The signal model is richer than the one ASLPrep fits, so clean pairs carry realistic model misfit: the bolus is dispersed (gamma kernel), label in tissue relaxes with tissue T1, and every image carries physiological noise proportional to its static signal (a global scale and a smooth field) on top of slightly smoothed thermal noise. Without dispersion and with tissue T1 equal to blood T1, the simulator matches ASLPrep's model to within 0.14%.

Four protocols span the range of repeats per delay: an HCP-Aging-like 2D protocol without background suppression (43 pairs; slice timing included), and 3D background-suppressed protocols with four, two and one pair per delay. Corrupted pairs get one of four artifacts with random severity: residual motion of the label image (0.3–1.5 mm and degrees), an intensity change in a slab of slices (2D) or the whole image (3D), partial labeling failure (30–100% of the signal lost), or 2–5 times the thermal noise. Errors are measured against fits to the noise-free signal, so the model's misfit to the simulated physics is not counted.

A change made after the first run

In the first full simulation, the same-delay correction was applied whenever at least one other pair shared the delay. With two pairs per delay, that made the hybrid detector worse than the model alone: each pair was predicted partly from its only neighbor, so a corrupted pair raised its clean neighbor's residual. The rule above (at least three other pairs) follows from the robustness of the median, but it was chosen after seeing those results, so it is reported here. Every number on this page is from the second run with the revised rule, except the first-run rows below, for the protocols the change affects.

Single-delay data

The kinetic model has nothing to contribute with one delay. ATT cannot be identified, and the best fit is a scaled average of the pairs, so the model's prediction reduces to the run's mean (or, with the robust start, median) ΔM. That is the same-delay median predictor (κ = 0) above. The scoring still applies: noise-standardized residual energy, the smoothed energy for spatially coherent artifacts, the amplitude test for labeling failures, and robust z-scores across pairs with refitting. Applied to single-delay data, this is a leave-one-out, median-referenced pair detector in the family of Tan et al.'s z-score rule and SCORE. In these simulations the same-delay median variant caught $empirical_tpr of corrupted pairs, against $hyb_tpr for the hybrid, and single-delay runs usually have far more repeats than one delay of a multi-delay run, which helps it. Whether it beats SCORE and SCRUB, which ASLPrep already has, is untested here. The parallel asl-outlier-eval study already benchmarks single-delay rejection on real runs, and these scores would fit there as one more method.
